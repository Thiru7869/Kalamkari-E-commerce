1 · What this site is

A storefront and long-form editorial site for a family workshop in Srikalahasti, Andhra Pradesh, that paints Srikalahasti Kalamkari entirely by hand — bamboo kalam, fermented iron black, madder red, vat indigo. The site's commercial job is to sell one-of-one pieces. Its rhetorical job is harder and more important: to establish, before anything is sold, that this is not the block- or screen-printed cloth the word "Kalamkari" now usually refers to in a marketplace listing.

That single claim shapes the architecture. The 23-step process page, the hand-versus-printed comparison, the dye index and the family chapters are not marketing decoration; they are the evidence for the claim, and the site is largely built to carry them. This work was therefore done as remediation and refinement inside the existing design language, not a redesign — the layout structure, information architecture, editorial voice and content model are unchanged throughout.

3 · The overflow bugs, and why they were invisible

The brief described content "spilling slightly to the right". Measurement found two different failures, and the more serious one did not scroll at all.

3.1 — The hero was clipping its own text

The hero section carries overflow-hidden for its 3D canvas. Behind that rule, the hero banner's six slide-tick buttons were flex-1 — which sets flex: 1 1 0% but says nothing about how far an item may shrink. min-width still defaults to auto, so a flex item will not go below its own min-content, and each tick's label carried truncate, which includes white-space: nowrap and therefore makes its min-content the entire untruncated label.

Measurement at 390px viewportBeforeAfter
Tick row min-content width456px— (shrinks)
Hero section.scrollWidth (box is 390px)476px390px
Content clipped and unreachable86px0px
Slide ticks laid out off-screen2 of 60 of 6

Because the section clipped rather than scrolled, document.documentElement.scrollWidth measured clean. What the visitor actually saw was every line of the hero paragraph losing its last few letters — "in colours fermente" — and two of the six slide controls simply not existing.

Fix: min-w-0 on the tick buttons, so truncate can finally do the job it was added for; and the labels hidden below sm, where ~50px each would render "Blouse pieces" as "Blo…". The accessible name is preserved on the button's aria-label, and the active slide's name is already set over the panel above.

3.2 — Pagination pushed /shop past a 320px viewport

"Previous" and "Next" are roughly 99px and 74px of unbreakable text. With the numbered buttons at 44px each, the row's min-content exceeded the 280px available inside the container at 320px, pushing the document 12px wide. Collapsed to icon-only below sm, with sr-only names and the 44px targets intact.

3.3 — Every input carried a hidden 223px floor

An <input> with no size attribute defaults to size="20" — about 223px of intrinsic width. That figure still counts toward a flex or grid container's min-content even when the input itself is w-full and perfectly happy to be 40px wide. The container refuses to shrink, and the overflow then surfaces somewhere else entirely.

Fix: defaulted size={1} at the Input primitive rather than at each call site, so every input on the site is immune. It remains overridable.

overflow-x: hidden was not added, and must not be. The project's own conventions record that such a rule previously hid a broken header rather than preventing one, and that overflow on the body makes it a scroll container — which breaks position: sticky on descendants and changes what scrollIntoView does. Section 3.1 is precisely why: a clipping rule converts a visible bug into an invisible one. Every fix above addresses the cause.

3.4 — The regression harness

scripts/audit-layout.mjs (npm run audit:layout) drives headless Chromium over ten routes at 320 / 375 / 390 / 430px and reports both failure modes: documents that scroll sideways, and elements that clip their own text. It reports the shallowest offending element rather than the deepest, because the shallow one is the cause and everything under it is a symptom. It exits non-zero so it can gate a build.

Two exclusions matter, and both were learned from false positives during this work: sr-only elements (a 1×1px box hiding a whole sentence is the point of the pattern), and anything with text-overflow: ellipsis — clipping with a mark is a decision, clipping silently is the bug.

4 · The sizing problem

4.1 — Root cause: a clamp with no intercept

The brief reported that components looked too large on both desktop and mobile, and larger still after deploying. The cause was one malformed pattern repeated across the whole type scale:

display-xl: clamp(3rem, 8.6vw, 6rem)

A clamp() whose middle term is a bare vw value has a flat floor. 8.6vw does not reach 3rem until a 558px viewport — so every phone on earth received an identical 48px headline, with no scaling whatsoever across the entire mobile range. On a 390px screen that spent four lines and 196px of vertical space on six words. This is also why the problem appeared worse "in production": it is not a build difference, it is that the scale simply stops responding below 558px regardless of environment.

Every size is now a genuine linear ramp — Nvw + Crem — solved through two points: a comfortable size at 320px and the intended size at 1440px. The ceilings came down too; the desktop headline was also larger than the writing beneath it deserved.

5 · A latent bug found along the way

cn() was silently deleting font sizes. This was already live on the deployed site and was not part of the brief. It is the single highest-impact fix in this batch.

tailwind-merge resolves conflicts by class group, and text-* is two groups sharing one prefix: font-size and text-color. It separates them using a list of known size keys — the stock Tailwind scale, which has never heard of display-xl. So cn('text-display-xl', 'text-cotton-50') filed the size as a colour, found it in conflict with the real colour, and returned only the colour.

The class then vanished and the heading inherited 16px from the body. It fails silently, and only through cn() — a plain className="text-display-xl text-cotton-50" string is never merged and renders correctly, which is exactly why it survived review. It appears only where a component merges a caller's classes.

ElementRenderedIntended
two-crafts section <h2> (live on production)16px25px
Home <h1>, once wrapped for the entrance animation16px37px

The two-crafts heading was rendering smaller than the body copy underneath it on the live site. extendTailwindMerge in src/lib/utils.ts now declares every custom fontSize key, and src/lib/utils.test.ts pins each one against a merged text colour — so adding a key to tailwind.config.ts without adding it there fails the suite rather than quietly shrinking a heading.

There is no green dye in this workshop. 7 · The welcome block

The block is the first thing on the site and its job is to be a doorway, not a banner. A visitor arriving here has usually just left a marketplace listing selling a printed polyester saree as "Kalamkari".

7.1 — Image → film

The still portrait has become a short muted film. The reasoning is specific rather than decorative: a photograph of a person at a table and a moving hand drawing a line are not the same argument, and the line is the entire claim of this site. The existing /video/opening.mp4 asset is reused rather than re-cut.

PropertyBehaviour
PlaybackMuted, looping, playsInline, autoplay attempted on mount.
Reduced motionThe file is never requested. The poster frame stands in its place and the block is otherwise identical. The media query is subscribed, not sampled — a visitor who changes the setting while the page is open gets the change.
Loadingpreload="metadata". The poster carries the first impression, and a 3MB download must never compete with the hero image for LCP.
Autoplay refusalIf play() rejects — data saver, battery saver, a policy muting did not satisfy — the control resolves to a visible play button rather than a frozen frame with no way in.
AccessibilityThe <video> is aria-hidden and untabbable; the always-painted poster carries the alt text, so the frame is described once rather than twice. The toggle is a 44px control with an sr-only label.
FittingAspect ratio is fixed at both sizes, so the block's height never depends on the file.

Why it fits without extra scrolling

Two constraints do the work. The ratio is 21:9 on a phone, where the film sits above the greeting and every pixel of height it takes is a pixel the welcome loses; and 4:5 from sm, where it is a column beside the words and a letterbox would leave a stripe floating in white space. The film's column is capped in rem (10rem, 11.5rem at lg) and never in fractions — given a 1fr column it grew to 260px on a laptop and the greeting beside it became its caption, inverting what the block is for.

7.2 — Social links Needs your handles

The social row is built, styled and wired into the block, directly under the invitation. It reads through the existing socialLinks() and whatsappHref() helpers, which is the site's established rule for every contact channel: an unpublished channel renders as nothing.

It currently renders nothing, by design. site.social.instagram, .facebook, .youtube and site.contact.whatsapp are all empty strings in src/content/site.ts. Placeholder URLs were explicitly not invented: on a live storefront a dead social link is worse than an absent one, because the visitor who taps it concludes the shop is abandoned. The "Find us" label is inside the same conditional, since an empty heading with nothing under it is its own kind of broken.

To switch it on

Edit src/content/site.ts only — no component changes are needed anywhere:

social: {
  instagram: 'https://instagram.com/<handle>',
  facebook:  'https://facebook.com/<page>',
  youtube:   'https://youtube.com/@<channel>',
},
contact: {
  whatsapp: '919876543210',   // digits only, country code first
},

The icons — Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, WhatsApp — appear immediately in the welcome block and in the footer, each as a 40px target with an sr-only name. Channels left empty stay hidden individually, so you can publish one without publishing all four.

8 · Motion

Everything below animates transform and opacity only, so it stays on the compositor. Nothing animates layout properties, and nothing writes scroll position into React state.

MechanismSpecNotes
Page-load entrance
Entrance		 opacity 0→1, translateY 14px→0
700ms · cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)
70ms stagger		 A stylesheet animation, not a component with behaviour — no use client, nothing to hydrate. animation-fill-mode: both is load-bearing: with forwards, a delayed animation sits at its normal state through the delay then snaps to the first keyframe, so a staggered row would flash fully-formed and blink out before arriving.
Scroll reveal
RevealOnScroll (existing)		 opacity 0→1, translateY 20px→0, scale .988→1
700ms		 IntersectionObserver, self-disconnecting. Deliberately skips above-the-fold content — it hides the element in an effect, and an effect runs after a paint.
Parallax
Parallax		 translate3d, offset = −(distance from viewport centre) × speed. Default speed 0.08. One rAF and one scroll listener for the whole page; reads batched ahead of writes; membership gated by IntersectionObserver; listeners refcounted so the first component to unmount cannot take the scroll handler away from the rest.
Button press translateY −1px on hover, 0 on :active, 150ms ease-out Explicit transition property list rather than transition-all, so a hover can never accidentally animate a width. The transform is motion-safe; the colour change is not, because it carries the information.
Page transition (existing)8px rise, 400ms Entrance only, no exit — an exit has to delay the navigation to play. Runs after the new page has committed.
Card hover (existing)image scale(1.05), 700msUnder motion-safe:.

The LCP rule

Entrance step={0} carries no delay at all, and the hero <h1> uses it. An element still at opacity: 0 is not eligible to be the Largest Contentful Paint, so a 300ms stagger on the wrong element buys a pleasant arrival and pays for it in the metric that decides whether anyone waits to see the page. The eyebrow leads visually but costs nothing to hold back, so it takes the later step instead.

Reduced motion

The site's existing contract is preserved and extended: decorative motion stops, but scroll-driven content still resolves to its final state — a visitor who asks for less motion must not lose information. Entrance applies its class only under motion-safe:, so content is simply present rather than hidden. Parallax is never mounted. The welcome film never downloads. Media queries are subscribed rather than sampled once on mount.

14 · Notes on what was deliberately not done

  • The 60-30-10 ratios were not re-tuned. They are already AA-verified, documented, and were re-struck once before after the site had been read rather than looked at. Widening the palette is additive; re-balancing a working measured system would have risked its contrast guarantees for no gain.
  • No layout was redesigned. Every change is size, spacing, colour or motion. Section order, component structure, information architecture and editorial voice are untouched.
  • No copy claims were invented. The site makes a specific factual claim on every page, and the project's editorial rule is that a feature the site does not have is never described and a fact that is not known is absent rather than guessed. The green added to the palette is documented as indigo over a yellow ground because that is how Kalamkari green is actually made.
  • No overflow-x: hidden. See §3.
